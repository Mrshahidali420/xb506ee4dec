## Sad / emotional

- Open with a charming, not-sad hook (45 s of the child's gift) so the later loss costs more; sadness arrives by one reveal line. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Role reversal is the tear peak: the child comforts the crying adult. Keep that beat clean and uncut. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- At the peak the narrator goes plain (facts only) and lets faces and music carry it; the explicit feeling line comes AFTER the peak. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Stack small kindnesses from the innocent character against unfair treatment, one setup and payoff per beat, three in a row. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Sad music: soft solo piano, no beat (The Rain, Joe Hisaishi), only on the sad stretches, starting a few seconds before the sad action; setup scenes carry none. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Sad recaps can use third person, present tense (the funny refs use past). [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]

## Funny

- Hook is the whole absurd premise or one flat fact, in one or two short lines, then straight into events. [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, 2]
- One event per sentence, 7-20 words, plain verbs; the narrator never explains the joke or the feeling. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, 3]
- Jokes are deadpan and unannounced; judgment lines are rare and dry. [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, 2]
- Music: sparse bed for the setup, one driving beat track for the run of gags, one light bouncy swap at the payoff. Do not change tracks per scene. [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, 2]
- End on the punchline, or mid-scene on a cut-off line that points at the next part; no sign-off. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, 3]
- Pace is 155-190 words per minute. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, 3]
