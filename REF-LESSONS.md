## Sad / emotional

- Open with a charming, not-sad hook (45 s of the child's gift) so the later loss costs more; sadness arrives by one reveal line. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Role reversal is the tear peak: the child comforts the crying adult. Keep that beat clean and uncut. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- At the peak the narrator goes plain (facts only) and lets faces and music carry it; the explicit feeling line comes AFTER the peak. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Stack small kindnesses from the innocent character against unfair treatment, one setup and payoff per beat, three in a row. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Sad music: soft solo piano, no beat (The Rain, Joe Hisaishi), only on the sad stretches, starting a few seconds before the sad action; setup scenes carry none. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]
- Sad recaps can use third person, present tense (the funny refs use past). [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]

## Funny

- Hook is the whole absurd premise or one flat fact, in one or two short lines, then straight into events (funny-ref1: a superpower plus its price, in 12 s). STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, funny-ref1, 3] [fits: full episode, promo, Short]
- One event per sentence, 7-20 words, plain verbs; the narrator never explains the joke or the feeling. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, intrigue-ref1, funny-ref1 (3-15 words, present tense), 5]
- Jokes are deadpan and unannounced; judgment lines are rare and dry. [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, 2]
- Music: sparse bed for the setup, one driving beat track for the run of gags, one light bouncy swap at the payoff. Do not change tracks per scene. [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, 2]
- End on the punchline, or mid-scene on a cut-off line that points at the next part; no sign-off. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, intrigue-ref1 (ends on a half-heard line, no verdict), funny-ref1 (ends on the stitched back and a vision memory), 5]
- Pace is 155-190 words per minute. STRONG [seen in: funny-A, funny-B, cry-ref1, intrigue-ref1 (185), 4] Outlier: funny-ref1 runs 208 wpm with 8.4 cuts per 10 s (rapid Short-style); keep 155-190 for full episodes, 208 only for a Short that must fit under 2:20. [fits: Short]

## Thriller

- Hook is an open question with no answer yet ("a detective made an unexpected discovery"), answered 5 s later; re-use the same shape for the next turn (0:00 and 1:30). [seen in: intrigue-ref1, 1]
- Deduction ladder: one clue, one door closed every 3-5 s until one suspect is left, so the viewer plays detective. [seen in: intrigue-ref1, 1]
- Plant a physical clue early (bite marks, fish smell) and pay it off late by tying it to an earlier scene (bag smell 2:05 = bags on the victims 2:10). Not only thrillers: funny-ref1 orders an air freshener with no reason at 1:47 and pays it 11 s later when the intruder arrives. [seen in: intrigue-ref1, funny-ref1, 2] [fits: any type with a reveal]
- Never state guilt; end after the arrest on a half-heard line that suggests a different crime. [seen in: intrigue-ref1, 1]
- Reasoning ladder keeps the original's own order of elimination and drops the weakest rung (plumbers merged into electricians, 5 trades to 4) and the thinnest clue (no wood shavings on victims). [seen in: intrigue-ref1, 1]
- Music plan (measured, Demucs): silent for the first 8 s, bed in at about 8 dB under the voice, dips to 12-14 dB under on reasoning beats, almost out (20-34 dB under) for the dread scene, swells back to about 6 dB under at the final cut instead of fading. [seen in: intrigue-ref1, 1]

## Conversion (original to recap)

- The narrator writes the motive and the other person's feelings ("never told his wife", "doesn't blame her") over faces; the original only shows the action. [seen in: cry-ref1, 1]

- Reorder so the payoff comes before the consequence: the villain's exposure (original 26:35) is told before the executives' verdict (original 23:44), so the viewer gets the cheer first. [seen in: funny-ref1, 1] [fits: promo, Short]
